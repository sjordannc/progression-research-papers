# Research papers by Scott A. Jordan

This repository collects research manuscripts on relational clocks, progression geometry, matter and optical coupling, and possible modifications of gravitational or inertial dynamics. The manuscripts are shared for reading and discussion; inclusion does not imply peer review or empirical validation.

## Research direction

**Can observable relations among physical clocks and conserved exchanges explain temporal structure without assuming an independently flowing time?**

My current direction treats progression as a relational property of physical evolution. Clocks measure how physical changes accumulate along specified histories, and comparisons require a stated preparation, calibration, and event-pairing or signal protocol. Time is the operational measure of those relationships.

The central question is whether energy expressed relative to a local clock can be related to a conserved description through a weight derived from physical dynamics. The task is to specify the clocks, their interactions, the environment, and the symmetry or balance principle that determines both the weight and the conserved quantity. Local energy measurements remain valid in their stated reference; comparing energies across environments requires care about those references.

Read the [research-direction note](RESEARCH_DIRECTION.md) for the proposed formulation, its relationship to standard physics, and criteria for progress.

### How this direction relates to the existing papers

The collection explores several approaches to this question. Some papers introduce a progression field or adopt a complete geometric description; others study relational observables or a conditional reconstruction of geometry. These are distinct models with different assumptions. The current direction does not require a fundamental progression field, while leaving open whether such a field is useful as an effective description.

Three strands provide the clearest starting point:

1. **Finite relational clocks.** The connected Jacobi model constructs clock comparisons in one constrained mechanical system and identifies which changes are observable. It also gives a stable finite mechanism that induces organization-dependent clock inertias. This establishes a concrete model of relational transport, with explicit preparation and regularity conditions.
2. **Conditional geometric reconstruction.** The hypersurface-deformation-algebra paper investigates how local relational evolution could require a common geometry. Under stated integrability, locality, calibration, and canonical assumptions, it reconstructs Einstein geometrodynamics within the tested class and constrains matter characteristic cones.
3. **Dynamical consistency and alternatives.** The geometry, collective-loading, optics, interaction-complexity, and spectral-inertia papers investigate particular completions and their limitations. They supply calculations and consistency tests; they do not collectively establish a single completed theory.

An important constraint comes from the finite clock model: **a common fractional response cancels from local pairwise clock comparisons**. A universal progression effect therefore requires additional operational structure, such as propagation, spatial relations, or boundary references. An observable difference between two model clocks is not by itself a demonstration of universal gravitational time dilation.

### The next calculation

The next target is a physically specified system containing two calibrated clocks, an interaction or signal linking their records, and an environment with its own dynamics. The model should:

- Define its action and conserved charge or complete balance law before interpreting either clock as time.
- Derive the clock correlations and any energy-conversion weight from the same dynamics, including interaction and environmental exchanges.
- Preserve observable predictions when the reference clock is changed.
- Determine whether different clock mechanisms share the same relation after ordinary mechanism-specific shifts are accounted for.
- Recover the stationary weak-field gravitational clock ratio without inserting its potential dependence into the weight in advance.

Recovering an established relation from more primitive assumptions would be an explanatory result. A change of clock labels alone would be a reformulation. Any new empirical claim requires a calculated difference in dimensionless observables and comparison with measurements.
